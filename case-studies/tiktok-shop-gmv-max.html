Context

A new campaign type with most of the dials removed

The client was a leading B2C beauty and personal-care brand in Indonesia selling through TikTok Shop. I ran the account as Senior Digital Planner at Omnicom Media.

GMV Max is TikTok's automated campaign type for Shop sellers. The advertiser supplies a catalogue, a budget and a target return; TikTok's system handles targeting, bidding and creative selection across placements. It is philosophically the same bet as Google Performance Max — hand control to the algorithm in exchange for reach and simplicity.

That trade changes the job. There are no audiences to refine and no bids to adjust. Performance is shaped almost entirely by the quality of the inputs: the catalogue, the creative pool, the product selection, and the target itself.

Challenge

A 7.0x target is high enough to starve the campaign

Seven times return is a demanding target for beauty and personal care, a category where discounting is constant and margins are thin. Automated systems respond to a target that aggressive by simply reducing delivery — they will not spend into inventory they cannot clear at the required efficiency.

So the risk was not overspending. It was the opposite: a campaign that quietly under-delivers, hits the efficiency target on a fraction of the intended budget, and produces very little actual revenue while appearing to succeed on the headline metric.

Strategy

Manage the inputs, not the campaign

01

Curate which products enter the catalogue

Under a high return target, low-margin and slow-moving SKUs actively harm the campaign — they absorb delivery without clearing the efficiency bar. Restricting the catalogue to products that could realistically carry a 7.0x return was the single highest-leverage decision available.

02

Keep the creative pool deep and refreshed

An automated system can only choose from what it is given. A thin creative pool caps performance no matter how good the algorithm is, and TikTok creative fatigues faster than on any other platform. Feeding a steady stream of new assets is maintenance, not a campaign phase.

03

Treat the ROAS target as a variable

The target is an input to the algorithm, not just a scorecard. Set too high it suppresses delivery; set too low it buys low-quality volume. Monitoring the relationship between target, delivery and actual return each month is the closest thing GMV Max has to a bid strategy.

04

Respect the platform calendar

TikTok Shop conversion rates swing hard around platform campaign dates and payday cycles. Weighting budget toward those windows lets the same target be met with far less strain on the rest of the month.

Execution

The monthly operating rhythm

Phase 1 · Foundation

Catalogue hygiene before spend

Audited product data completeness, imagery and pricing accuracy across the Shop catalogue. In an automated catalogue campaign, a broken or incomplete product record is not a minor issue — it is an invisible performance ceiling.

Phase 2 · Selection

Narrow to products that can carry the target

Grouped SKUs by margin and historical sell-through, and scoped the campaign to those capable of supporting a 7.0x return rather than exposing the full catalogue.

Phase 3 · Feed & observe

Supply creative, then let it settle

Automated campaigns need a learning period, and intervening too early destroys the signal they are building. The discipline is supplying good inputs and then resisting the urge to touch the campaign daily — a genuine behavioural shift from manual buying.

Phase 4 · Monthly review

Adjust target, budget and catalogue together

Each month: read return against delivery, identify whether the constraint was the target, the creative pool or the product mix, adjust the relevant input, and weight the next month's budget toward the platform's campaign dates.

The insight

Automation moves the work upstream — it does not remove it

The instinct when a platform automates bidding is to assume there is less to do. The opposite is true: the work relocates. Time that used to go into audience construction and bid adjustment goes into catalogue quality, product selection and creative supply instead.

The second lesson is that a return target is a lever, not a verdict. A campaign quietly hitting 7.0x on a third of its budget is failing, not succeeding — and that failure is invisible if you only read the headline efficiency metric.

FAQ

Common questions

What is TikTok Shop GMV Max?

GMV Max is TikTok's automated campaign type for TikTok Shop sellers. Instead of running separate campaigns for product ads, video ads and live shopping, the advertiser supplies a product catalogue, a budget and a target return, and TikTok's system handles targeting, bidding and creative selection across placements to maximise gross merchandise value. It is philosophically TikTok's equivalent of Google Performance Max: automation-first, with fewer manual controls exposed to the buyer.

How is GMV Max different from manual TikTok ads campaigns?

The difference is where the operator's effort goes. In a manual campaign you control audiences, placements and bids directly. In GMV Max those are automated, so you influence performance indirectly through the inputs the system consumes: catalogue quality and product data completeness, the creative pool available, the target return and budget, and which products are grouped together. Managing GMV Max is closer to managing a feed and a creative pipeline than to managing bids.

What ROAS target is realistic for TikTok Shop GMV Max?

It varies by category, market and margin structure, so no single number applies universally. In this beauty and personal-care engagement in Indonesia the brand set a demanding 7.0x monthly target, which required tight control over which products were allowed into the campaign. Set the target too high and the campaign starves for delivery; set it too low and it buys low-quality volume. The target is itself an optimisation variable, not a fixed constraint.
