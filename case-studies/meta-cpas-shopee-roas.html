Context

A brand selling through someone else's storefront

The client was a leading B2C beauty and personal-care brand in Indonesia, selling primarily through marketplace platforms rather than its own e-commerce site. I worked on the account as Senior Digital Planner at Omnicom Media, responsible for planning and optimising the brand's paid social investment.

Marketplace-first selling creates a specific measurement problem. When the transaction happens on Shopee rather than on the brand's own domain, the brand cannot place its own pixel on the checkout. Without conversion signal, ad platforms optimise blind — and the brand cannot prove which ad drove which sale.

Challenge

Prospecting budget was buying attention, not sales

The category is crowded and heavily discount-driven, which pushes acquisition costs up during every marketplace campaign date. Spending against broad cold audiences produced traffic but weak return — the brand was paying full price to reach people with no demonstrated intent to buy.

Meanwhile the most valuable audience was already sitting unused: shoppers who had bought from the brand on the marketplace before and were approaching their natural repurchase point.

Strategy

Four moves that shifted the economics

01

Adopt CPAS to recover the conversion signal

Collaborative Ads gave the brand access to marketplace catalogue and conversion data it could not otherwise see. That single change turned an unmeasurable channel into an optimisable one — every subsequent decision depended on it.

02

Concentrate budget on a 60-day buyer window

Rather than spreading spend across cold prospecting, the majority of budget went to shoppers who had purchased within the previous 60 days. That window matched the category's replenishment cycle: long enough to catch repurchase intent, short enough to exclude decayed audiences.

03

Write creative per segment, not per campaign

Each audience segment received messaging matched to its relationship with the brand. Recent buyers saw replenishment and bundle messaging; category browsers saw proof and product benefit. One generic creative across all segments would have diluted every one of them.

04

Make the discount visible inside the creative

In a discount-driven marketplace category, the offer is the message. A/B tests consistently showed product images carrying the visible discount amount outperformed clean product shots — the price advantage had to be legible before the click, not after it.

Execution

How it ran, phase by phase

Phase 1 · Setup

Catalogue and partnership plumbing

Established the CPAS partnership with the marketplace, validated catalogue feed accuracy, and confirmed conversion events were reporting correctly before any budget went live. Feed errors at this stage silently cap performance for the entire flight.

Phase 2 · Segment

Audience build by recency

Built retargeting audiences segmented by recency and engagement depth, with the 60-day buyer pool as the priority tier. Each segment was sized to stay large enough for stable delivery without collapsing into the next tier.

Phase 3 · Test

Creative A/B against the offer variable

Ran structured creative tests isolating one variable at a time — discount visibility, product framing, copy angle — so that a winning ad could be explained rather than merely observed.

Phase 4 · Scale

Reallocate toward what proved out

Budget shifted weekly toward the segment-and-creative pairs delivering the strongest return, with spend pulled forward around marketplace campaign dates when conversion rates naturally peaked.

The optimisation loop

Weekly cycle: read segment-level ROAS → identify the weakest spend concentration → reallocate to the strongest performing pair → queue the next creative test → repeat. Nothing was left running on autopilot for more than a week.

Why it worked

The audience was never the problem — the signal was

The brand did not need a bigger audience. It needed to see which people were already close to buying, and CPAS was the mechanism that made them visible.

Once the signal existed, the winning move was almost boringly simple: spend disproportionately on people who had already bought, and put the offer where they could see it before clicking. Most of the 6.18x came from concentration, not cleverness.

FAQ

Common questions

What is Meta CPAS (Collaborative Ads)?

Meta CPAS lets a brand run Meta ads that link directly to its products on a retailer's marketplace, such as Shopee or Tokopedia. The retailer shares catalogue and conversion signal with the brand, so the brand can retarget shoppers and measure sales it would otherwise be unable to attribute. It is the standard solution for brands that sell through marketplaces rather than their own website.

What is a good ROAS for a Meta CPAS campaign?

For beauty and personal-care brands on Southeast Asian marketplaces, roughly 2x to 3x is a common working benchmark. This campaign reached 6.18x over February–April 2024 — about double the benchmark — by concentrating budget on recent buyers instead of cold prospecting audiences.

How long should a CPAS retargeting window be?

Here a 60-day window worked best, because it matched the replenishment cycle for beauty and personal-care products: long enough to capture shoppers ready to repurchase, short enough to exclude lapsed buyers whose intent had decayed. The right window is category-dependent — derive it from the product's actual repurchase cycle rather than copying a default.
